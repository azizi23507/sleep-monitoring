Sleep Quality Standards

Scientific standards used for sleep quality assessment

Temperature (18-22°C)

Optimal Range: 18-22°C (64-72°F)

Impact: Core body temperature drops during sleep. Cool room temperature facilitates this natural process.

Scoring:

  • 18-22°C: Optimal (full points)
  • 16-18°C or 22-24°C: Acceptable (reduced points)
  • <16°C or >24°C: Poor (minimal points)

References:

  • National Sleep Foundation - Best Temperature for Sleep
  • Okamoto-Mizuno, K., & Mizuno, K. (2012). Effects of thermal environment on sleep and circadian rhythm. Journal of Physiological Anthropology, 31(1), 14.

Humidity (40-60%)

Optimal Range: 40-60%

Impact: Proper humidity prevents dry airways and promotes comfortable breathing during sleep.

Scoring:

  • 40-60%: Optimal (full points)
  • 30-40% or 60-70%: Acceptable (reduced points)
  • <30% or >70%: Poor (minimal points)

References:

  • American Academy of Sleep Medicine - Sleep Environment Guidelines
  • Arens, E., & Zhang, H. (2006). The skin's role in human thermoregulation and comfort. Thermal and Moisture Transport in Fibrous Materials, 560-602.

Sound Level (<40dB)

Optimal Range: <40dB (Quiet library level)

Impact: Noise disrupts sleep stages and prevents deep, restorative sleep.

Scoring:

  • <40dB: Optimal (full points)
  • 40-55dB: Acceptable (reduced points)
  • 55-70dB: Disruptive (low points)
  • >70dB: Very disruptive (minimal points)

References:

  • WHO Environmental Noise Guidelines for the European Region (2018)
  • Basner, M., et al. (2014). Auditory and non-auditory effects of noise on health. The Lancet, 383(9925), 1325-1332.

Motion Detection (Minimal Movement)

Optimal: Fewer movements during sleep

Impact: Excessive movement indicates sleep fragmentation and reduced sleep quality.

Scoring:

  • 0-5 movements: Optimal (full points)
  • 6-15 movements: Moderate (reduced points)
  • 16-30 movements: Restless (low points)
  • >30 movements: Very restless (minimal points)

References:

  • Ancoli-Israel, S., et al. (2003). The role of actigraphy in the study of sleep and circadian rhythms. Sleep, 26(3), 342-392.
  • Sadeh, A. (2011). The role and validity of actigraphy in sleep medicine: an update. Sleep Medicine Reviews, 15(4), 259-267.

Overall Sleep Quality Classification

Scoring System: Combined weighted score from all environmental factors (0-100)

Classification:

  • Good Sleep: Score 70-100 (Optimal environmental conditions)
  • Poor Sleep: Score 0-69 (Suboptimal environmental conditions)

Note: This system evaluates environmental factors only. Clinical sleep disorders require professional medical assessment.

FHIR API - Healthcare Interoperability

About FHIR: Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR) is an international standard for electronic health information exchange.

This system implements FHIR R4 Observation resources, enabling integration with hospital systems and electronic health records.

Available FHIR Endpoints:

  • Get All Observations: GET http://localhost:3000/api/fhir/Observation
  • Filter by Device: GET http://localhost:3000/api/fhir/Observation?patient=Device/pi-001
  • Filter by Type (Temperature): GET http://localhost:3000/api/fhir/Observation?code=CUSTOM-TEMP-001
  • Limit Results: GET http://localhost:3000/api/fhir/Observation?_count=10
  • Get Single Observation: GET http://localhost:3000/api/fhir/Observation/{id}

LOINC Codes Used:

  • CUSTOM-TEMP-001: Ambient Temperature
  • CUSTOM-HUM-001: Relative Humidity
  • CUSTOM-SOUND-001: Sound Level
  • CUSTOM-MOTION-001: Motion Detection

FHIR Resources:

  • FHIR R4 Specification
  • FHIR Observation Resource
  • LOINC - Logical Observation Identifiers Names and Codes

Note: Custom LOINC codes are used for this educational project. Production systems should apply for official LOINC codes through the Regenstrief Institute.
